# TILWEN — Voice & Claims Spec

Binding on all customer-facing copy: product pages, hero, nav, footer, rug-type
essays, glossary, motifs, regions, stories, emails, metadata, MidJourney captions.
If you are writing anything a visitor or a search engine will read, this governs it.

This exists because the same two mistakes keep recurring. Read it before writing.

---

## The core principle: the Queen of Pentacles

Tilwen is the house. It speaks with the calm authority of someone who owns her
harvest and does not need to prove it. It never chases, never compares, never
argues its own position, never asks to be believed.

Authority speaks about the SUBJECT — the wool, the dye, the loom, the region, the
work. It never speaks about Tilwen's standing in the market ("unique", "rare",
"the best", "we can prove it", "nothing else like it"). The moment copy reaches
for a claim about itself, it is arguing, and arguing is chasing.

State what is. Let the room decide.

---

## Two banned patterns (these are the recurring failures)

### 1. Insecure claim-making — the overclaim that creates liability
Tilwen sells ordinary, real, vintage rugs. That is the truth, and it is neither
disclosed nor faked (Cinderella at the ball does not announce she is a servant,
and does not claim to be a princess — she simply arrives with poise). But copy
must NEVER make a claim that a buyer could check and find false, or that invites
legal or reputational risk.

BANNED — uniqueness / rarity / superiority / scarcity:
- "one of a kind, and we can prove it" · "the one you'll never see in another house"
- "nothing else like it" · "unlike anywhere" · "the finest" · "exceptional [quality/wool/…]"
- "increasingly rare" · "almost never available" · "seldom available" · "rarely available"
- "the condition is exceptional" (condition lives in the grade, never the pitch)
- "this is why this piece is here" (defensive; delete)
- "some of the finest" · "technical peak" · any ranking Tilwen asserts about itself

ALLOWED — facts that happen to be strong, stated plainly:
- "one of the most isolated weaving traditions" (geography — a fact)
- "one of the most recognised Moroccan textiles of the last century" (history — a fact)
- "one of the most permanent natural dyes" (material fact)
- Attributed views, clearly marked as views ("you will be told…", "collectors read…")

Test: could a buyer stand in front of the rug, or another rug elsewhere, and catch
the sentence being false or boastful? If yes, cut it.

### 2. Modest hedging — playing small
Tilwen is the house, full stop. Never write it as new, small, humble, or emerging.
Never apologise for scale or position.

BANNED:
- "we're a new/small gallery" · "we hope" · "we try" · "we believe" · "humbly"
- "we're proud to" · "passion for" · "carefully curated / hand-picked with love"
- any framing that positions Tilwen as junior, aspiring, or seeking approval

The confidence is in the plainness, not in adjectives about ourselves.

---

## Emotion lives in the fact, not the adjective

Show, don't claim. Replace every reached-for superlative with the fact that earns it.
- NOT "the wool is exceptional" → "the altitude and cold grow a dense, long-staple fleece"
- NOT "of exceptional geometric precision" → "dense, worked at a fineness that takes real skill"
The fact is more authoritative than the adjective. Always.

---

## Also binding (carried from the wider rules)

- NO religion, NO politics — anywhere, ever. No "pre-Islamic", no colonial /
  resistance framing, no state politics, no deity/goddess language. Describe the
  object and the craft.
- NO invented meaning. Report symbolism as reported; name guesses as guesses;
  let the weaver's "it's the shape my mother used" stand beside the collector's story.
- NO price talk in reference content; value-as-concept is fine, actual numbers/
  "premium"/"price point" are not. Prices live on the product page only.
- "Amazigh (Berber)" on first mention per page; "Amazigh" thereafter. "Berber"
  owns titles/SEO because that is what people search.
- Reading text is full-strength ink at ≥1rem. Greys are for chrome only.
- Style: Strunk. Plain, warm, full sentences (not staccato). Scene-first where it
  is a story. Quiet endings. No AI-speak, no word salad, no personification of place.

---

## One-line summary for the writer in a hurry
Speak like the house that owns the harvest: describe the subject with plain
authority, claim nothing about yourself, promise nothing a buyer could disprove,
and never, ever play small.

## Story writing (the /stories voice)

Stories are not the reference pages. They are young, light, quick, funny where a
laugh is earned. The reader learns while having fun. Joy is the differentiator —
every other rug shop writes the same solemn artisan-hush; we don't.

Hard rules, learned the hard way:

1. **In medias res. No preamble.** Open inside the action, word one. The reader is
   already kneeling next to the weaver, already standing in front of the rug. Never
   step to the front of the room to explain the topic first. If the first sentence
   sets up the story instead of *being* the story, cut it.
   - LECTURE (banned): "You want the deep red, you wait three years. That's the deal with madder."
   - SCENE (correct): "She's got a knife in her hand and she's not cutting the plant. She's digging past it, down to the root, counting years while she does it."

2. **Only humans and animals are subjects.** A plant does not "die back", "sulk",
   "get fussy", or "wait". The WEAVER waits, digs, soaks. Personifying objects or
   plants is the mysticism reflex in a comic costume — it is banned even when it's
   trying to be funny.

3. **Show, don't teach.** The teaching arrives *while* the reader is in the scene
   (she learns the alum trick because the weaver is doing it; she learns the fade
   tell because the rug is in front of her). Never "here's how X works".

4. **Watch the em dashes.** Two per story is plenty. Use full stops.

5. **No clever-empty titles.** "Madder Doesn't Do Instant" is AI-clever and hollow.
   Title states something real: "The Three-Year Red".

6. **Every story is a different joke.** The only constant is no pomposity, no
   Wikipedia voice, no myth. Vary the humour, the angle, the shape.

Reference sample (the voice, correct): see "The Three-Year Red" —
scene-first, human subjects, the fade-tell taught inside the scene, a closing
line that's both the joke and the point ("waited longer than he did").
